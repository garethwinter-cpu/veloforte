The format: half gel, half chew

Before the flavours, the thing itself. It sits between a gel and a chew, and it fixes about half of what's wrong with chews.

Works

In the mouth

Smooth and set, with more body than a standard gel. Same family as Maurten's thicker gel but with more texture, and never actually grainy. Think of a chew that's already gone soft in a hot back pocket: you skip the chewing and go straight to the bit where it melts. Easier to get down on the bike than chews.

Works

Half now, half later

Take half, fold the wrapper over, put it back in your pocket. Try that with a normal gel and it leaks down your jersey. This is a real advantage you can show in five seconds of video, and it should be in the launch creative.

Half solved

Getting it out

With a tear-off tab, the first squeeze will be easy and most of the gel comes out with little effort. The last third needs a firm roll from the bottom, much firmer than a gel. That's two hands off the bars for a cyclist, or two hands working away mid-stride for a runner. Better than chews, not yet as easy as a gel.

Passed

The freezer test

Ellen's challenge. Half went in the freezer and I ate it the next morning. It didn't freeze solid. It stayed soft enough to squeeze and eat. Good news for winter rides and for anyone who leaves their fuel in a cold car overnight.

Sample 01

Lemon & Ginger

Version one of two
8/10
Would I buy it?Yes

My favourite tea, turned into fuel. I'm biased, and I'll say so.

Lemon and ginger is one of my favourite flavour combinations, full stop. It's the tea I drink most, so this sample started ahead before I'd opened it. It earned the lead anyway. Bright lemon arrives first, then the ginger turns up a beat later as warmth, not burn. It tastes like Veloforte: punchy and natural, with nothing from the sweet shop.

This was the first sample, so it's also where I met the texture. It's thicker than a standard gel, in the same family as Maurten's, and smoother than I expected. I loved it. I think it will divide people: some will welcome it, others will be put off. That's a hunch. Prove me wrong.

Keep

  • The lemon first, ginger second order
  • Ginger pitched as warmth, not heat
  • The natural flavour, nothing confected

Decide

  • Nothing to fix in the flavour
  • Sample 7 tastes almost the same. Choose one, or push the other far enough apart to earn a place (see sample 7)
Who it's for: Zenzero fans and anyone who already drinks lemon and ginger. It's the obvious bridge from the bar range to the new format.
Sample 02

Orange

Sweet, soft, a little mandarin
7/10
Would I buy it?Yes, with more zing

Lovely and polite. Veloforte isn't famous for polite.

I like this flavour and the texture. It's sweet, with a mandarin quality rather than straight orange, and that's a good thing. Orange is loved almost everywhere. It tastes of summer and it's very hard to dislike, so this is a natural crowd pleaser and a safe first purchase.

The problem is volume. Next to Citro it whispers. Veloforte is known for punch, and this should taste as if someone has just grated zest over it. Turn up the zest and the acidity to Citro's level for a real kick, and keep the mandarin sweetness underneath to round it off.

Keep

  • The mandarin sweetness
  • The texture
  • Its broad, crowd-pleasing appeal

Change

  • More zest and more acidity, benchmarked against Citro
  • Re-taste side by side with Citro before sign-off
Who it's for: new customers and mixed packs. It's the flavour nobody rejects, once it has some bite.
Sample 03

Vanilla

The neutral one
5/10
Would I buy it?Maybe

I was ready to hate it. The texture changed my mind. The flavour didn't, quite.

The surprise of the tasting. This had the best texture of the eight: smoother, and much more like a true jelly than a set gel. Whatever is different about this base, find out what it is and take it into the other flavours.

The flavour was pure nostalgia: the pink and white Chewits I ate as a kid, sitting in the park with my grandmother. (That flavour has since been discontinued.) Subtle vanilla and a lot of sweetness. It's a lovely memory, but it's a sweet-shop flavour, a long way from the punchy, natural taste people expect from Veloforte. I can't see it as a hero on the shelf.

Keep

  • The texture. Find out why it's smoother and use it across the range

Change

  • Drop it as a flavour, or
  • Reposition it as a plain, low-flavour option for long days when every other flavour gets too much
Who it's for: possibly ultra runners and long-distance riders who get tired of fruit flavours. Test before committing.
Sample 04 · Favourite

Grapefruit

The star of the tasting
9/10
Would I buy it?Yes

My favourite of the eight, and the best case for a new flavour in the range.

Sweet, sharp and zingy, with the slightly bitter edge that makes grapefruit taste grown-up. The texture was great. It tastes fresh in a way I'd still want two hours into a ride.

The bigger opportunity is the pack. Pink grapefruit is a beautiful fruit: blush-pink flesh, sunny rind. On a shelf or in a feed it would stop the scroll. The palette leans feminine, which could make it especially attractive to women who run, an audience many endurance brands still treat as an afterthought. That gives people a reason to buy before they've tasted it.

Keep

  • All of it: flavour, sweetness, zing, texture

Build on

  • Design the pack around the fruit's colours
  • Make it the hero of the launch creative
  • Seed it with women's running clubs and creators
Who it's for: runners, especially women's running communities, and anyone bored of orange and lemon.
Sample 05

Lemon

Solid, and a strategy question
8/10
Would I buy it?Yes

A crowd pleaser that raises the hardest commercial question of the eight.

A clean, bright lemon with great texture. There's nothing to fix in how it eats. The question is what it does to the range. There are already lemon chews and a lemon gel. A lemon jelly gel would make three. At what point does that stop growing the basket and start splitting it? Would I pick the lemon gel or the lemon jelly gel, or take both?

It's a good flavour. What I'd like to see is the data-backed reason for the flavour choices.

  1. Does it raise average order value, or swap one lemon product for another?
  2. Does it encourage repeat purchase?
  3. Does it get people exploring the range, or keep them inside lemon?
  4. How many current orders already contain both lemon gels and lemon chews? If that share is high, lemon is a flavour people stay loyal to across formats, and a third format extends it. If it's low, expect substitution.
Who it's for: existing lemon loyalists. Whether that's enough depends on the answers above.
Sample 06

Lime

Pleasant, and outclassed
4/10
Would I buy it?No

Nice enough. Not what I'd reach for when I'm working hard.

There's nothing wrong with it. But lime is greener and more bitter, closer to a cocktail than refreshment. Mid-effort, lemon and grapefruit both give the bright, sweet-sour hit you actually want. Lime sits between them and doesn't beat either.

Keep

  • Nothing specific worth carrying over

Change

  • Leave it out of the launch line-up
  • Put the development time into grapefruit and lemon
Sample 07

Lemon & Ginger

Version two of two
8/10
Would I buy it?Yes

Really good, and too close to version one to tell apart.

I struggled to tell this from sample 1. If I can't taste the difference, customers won't either, and two near-identical flavours just compete for the same shelf space. There are two clean ways forward.

Option A

  • Run a blind side-by-side with a wider panel
  • Launch the winner, drop the other

Option B

  • Push this one much further on ginger heat
  • Sell a gentle version and a fiery version as clearly different products
My lean: option A for launch. Keep the fiery idea for a later limited run if lemon and ginger sells.
Sample 08

Mint

Proven elsewhere, unproven here
4/10
Would I buy it?No

Nice, and there's proof mint can sell. I'm not convinced it's a Veloforte flavour.

Mint has real history in endurance food. Kendal Mint Cake has gone up mountains for more than a century, including on the 1953 Everest expedition, and Kendal Mint Co. has built a loyal following among fell runners, ultra runners and hikers with mint energy products. So the demand exists.

I'd still say no for launch, for three reasons. A big hit of mint feels odd to a lot of people when they're breathing hard, and it lingers into whatever you eat or drink next. It doesn't fit Veloforte's flavour story of fruit, nuts, citrus and spice. And in the UK, mint fuel already has an owner. Launching against Kendal means arriving second.

Where it could work

  • Hot climbs and long hill days, where cool and clean feels welcome
  • Ultra and hiking communities, where Kendal has already proved the appetite

Before saying yes

  • Test with an ultra or fell panel, not a general one
  • Find a Veloforte angle on mint rather than a copy of Kendal's
Who it's for: ultra runners, fell runners and hikers. A niche product at best, not a core one.

What it adds up to

The format, the controversy and the test every Veloforte product has to pass.

Love it or hate it

The texture will split people. Two reactions from the same kitchen:

Wow, this is nice.Me
Oh my god, that looks f***ing disgusting.Becky

I love that. Veloforte isn't a universal need. Everyone who trains needs fuel, but nobody needs Veloforte in particular. It's a nice-to-have, so being sensible and inoffensive won't win.

What can win is a product that gets people talking. Get the controversy right and you'll collect negative reactions, but just as many positive ones, and every reaction is a signal. The more people respond, the more the algorithms push you to new audiences. The conversation snowballs, and the love-or-hate question creates a curiosity gap: people try it to find out which side they're on.

To stand out you have to stand for something: rally a crowd of loyalists and accept that you'll divide the sceptics.

The Veloforte test

Every Veloforte product has to deliver on four beliefs. If it doesn't, frankly, it isn't a Veloforte product. Here's where the jelly gel stands after one tasting.

TextureStrongDistinctive and divisive. Use vanilla's smoother base everywhere.
FlavourStrongFour or five flavours are launch-ready. Orange needs more punch.
Natural and easy to digestUnknownNot testable at a kitchen table. Needs the ingredient list and field testing on long efforts.
PerformanceUnknownCarbs per serving and how it compares with gels and chews at race pace.

The format, in one line

It solves half the chew problem. Easy first squeeze, easy half-and-pocket, but scraping out the last third still takes a two-handed roll. A real improvement, not finished yet.

Questions for Lara and the team

I can build a proper go-to-market plan, but it should rest on evidence, not on my palate.

Product and customer

  1. What problem does this solve for the customer, or is it a test-and-learn? What gap in the market does it fill?
  2. Who is it for: runners, cyclists, or both?
  3. What switches people on to it, and what switches them off?
  4. What data or customer feedback supports it so far?

Competition

  1. How similar is it to Maurten's offer?
  2. Using Maurten as the reference point, what are the differences that matter?
  3. Where do texture, Veloforte flavour and digestibility together give us something Maurten can't claim?

Go-to-market

  1. How sophisticated is this market, and what does that mean for how we pitch it?
  2. How does it become a desirable or deliberately controversial part of the line-up?
  3. Who are the key people to seed it with, and how do we get social proof in front of buyers quickly?
  4. Any other thoughts the team has on launch.

To build an evidence-backed plan, I need to know three things about the audience

  1. What resonates with the Veloforte audience today
  2. What divides them
  3. What they have rejected
